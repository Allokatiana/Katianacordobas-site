Are We Creators of Our Life, or Do We Follow It?

There is a certain grace in learning to follow. We are so used to the idea of leading, of making things happen, of shaping the world to our will. But what if creating isn't about imposing our vision on reality, but about moving with it? What if it means listening before speaking, and feeling before acting?

I have been exploring this by singing and dancing with my husband. I'm used to singing and dancing on my own, moving freely and following my own rhythm. But now we are practicing something different. When he sings, I don't just sing my own part; I listen, I harmonize, and I shape my voice in relation to his. When we dance, he moves first, and I respond, not as someone passive, but as an active participant in what is unfolding. And in that adjusting, something new is created, not by force, but through presence.

Isn't that how life actually works?

Creating From What Is

Creation is a dialogue with life, not a monologue. We create from what already exists. We are born into a certain family, a particular culture, a specific moment in history. We can't change these facts instantly, but we can move within them.

When we struggle against what is, when we fight, reject or try to control reality, we create friction, resistance and suffering. But when we flow with what is, we begin to create with the raw material life has given us. This isn't passivity; it is an intelligent form of participation. It's the difference between a musician who forces a melody onto a song and one who listens first, then plays in harmony with the music that is already there.

The creative process isn't about demanding a different reality. It is about discovering what can be expressed through the reality that already exists.

The Feminine Flow of Creation

In many traditions, the feminine principle is associated with receptivity, adaptability and the ability to move with life rather than against it. This doesn't mean weakness or submission as we often imagine them. It means being deeply attuned to the present moment, and adjusting, shaping and creating in relationship with what already exists.

The masculine energy is structure, form and direction; it moves forward, initiates and sets boundaries. The feminine energy flows within that structure, filling the spaces, shaping itself around what is, and giving life to what has been set in motion. Together, they create something whole.

If we only live from the masculine, always leading and pushing forward, we become rigid and cut off from the living intelligence of the moment. If we only live from the feminine, always flowing and adjusting, we may lose our sense of agency and our ability to act decisively. But when we bring both together, we step into the true art of creation: moving with life while also shaping it.

Creating From Presence

This is why true creation happens in the present moment. Yes, we can have a vision of the future. We can imagine possibilities, set intentions and hold a direction in mind. But creation doesn't happen in the future; it happens now, in how we respond to what is in front of us.

A sculptor doesn't create from an idea alone, but from the stone in their hands. A dancer doesn't move in empty space, but with the rhythm, the music and the energy of the room. And in our lives, we don't create from fantasy; we create from what is.

The more present we are, the more aware we become of this process. When we heal, we no longer react unconsciously to life; we respond to it with awareness. We create not from old wounds or conditioned patterns, but from clarity, presence and a deep connection with what is.

Part of the Symphony

So what if, instead of always trying to control life, we practiced dancing with it? What if we saw creation not as something we do to the world, but something we do with it?

Being a conscious creator doesn't mean forcing life into a particular shape. It means moving with what life offers, and recognizing that we aren't separate from the great unfolding of existence, but part of it, like a musician in an orchestra, a wave in the ocean, a voice in a song.

Creation is happening all the time. The question is whether we are listening. Are we present enough to move with it, and willing to adjust, harmonize and shape our movements in relationship with what is already being expressed? Because when we are, something beautiful happens: we stop resisting life and begin to dance with it. And in that dance, we become creation itself.
