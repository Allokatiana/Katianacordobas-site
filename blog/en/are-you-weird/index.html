Are You Weird? That's Your Gift

Are you weird? Yes, I'm asking you seriously. Is there something in you that feels different, that you can't quite explain, that you have kept hidden because you thought no one would understand, or worse, that people would judge you for it? Because if so, I have something important to tell you: what you are hiding might be exactly your gift.

What Does It Mean to Be Weird?

Let me clear something up first. When I say weird, I don't mean it as an insult. I mean it as a way of describing something that doesn't fit neatly into a box, something the ordinary world doesn't quite know how to classify, because it comes from a more original source.

Being weird doesn't mean being broken. It means not having been tamed: something that hasn't been cut down to fit the mold, that has kept its original shape, unpolished and untranslated, without asking for permission.

Interestingly, what is most authentic in a person often shows up first as weirdness. Before it is recognized as a gift, it unsettles people. It surprises them, and it doesn't fit.

But there is something else worth saying: in some way, everyone is weird. Each person carries a uniqueness that is never repeated. Some people have simply learned to hide it better than others, and that is what we call being "normal."

We All Receive a Gift

I deeply believe that we all arrive in this life with a gift. But that gift doesn't always come as an obvious talent or a recognizable skill. Sometimes it shows up as sensitivity, as perception, as a very particular way of feeling the world that no one else feels in quite the same way.

The most fundamental gift we receive is simply being who we are. Our authenticity is the gift.

The problem is that very few people recognize it. Not because it doesn't exist, but because there are filters: layers of conditioning, fear, and messages we received growing up that told us, directly or indirectly, that's not something you show, that's too much, that's weird, better keep it hidden. And so, little by little, we covered the light.

A person who doesn't yet see their gift isn't missing it because they don't have one, but because there are still filters blocking their view. And that's okay; each path has its own timing. When those filters start to fall away, something opens, and what appears isn't new. It was always there.

What I Kept Hidden

I have my own weirdness, and I know it well.

One part of it is hard to put into words, which already says something. I can perceive the sound of emotions, of inner states, of what is alive inside a person. I tune into their energy, into what their body and their being are carrying, and from there I let that sound come through my voice. It isn't a technique I learned from a book; it is something that simply happens when I stop blocking it. That sound helps calm the nervous system, release what has been held tight, and bring harmony back where there was chaos. My hands follow naturally, moving with the sound as if they were part of it.

For a long time, I was afraid to show this. I thought people would think I was crazy, or that I believed I was someone special, or that it was simply too much.

And yet today, that is exactly what sits at the heart of my work. This weirdness is what allows me to accompany others through sound in a way that goes far beyond technique. Not in spite of being weird, but precisely because I am. If you would like to experience it, my videos can show it better than any written word, because there are things the body and the voice express more clearly than words.

The Filters That Dim the Light

Why do we hide who we are? Usually for one of these reasons. There is the fear of judgment: they'll think I'm crazy. The fear of seeming arrogant: they'll think I'm full of myself. The fear of responsibility: if I show this, people will expect more of me, and I'll have to live up to it. Or simply a kind of laziness about shining, because shining asks something of us. Authenticity asks us to embody it, to make choices and to be present.

Sometimes we even use humility as an excuse. We say "I'm nobody, there's nothing special about me," as if that were a virtue. But hiding who we are isn't humility; it is denial. And when we dim our light, we don't only hurt ourselves. We take something away from the world too.

The Lamp Isn't Meant to Be Hidden

Jesus said something that has stayed with me deeply: you don't light a lamp to hide it under a basket, but to put it where it can give light.

I understand it this way: when life, God or consciousness allows you to see a light in yourself, that light isn't only for your own relief. Yes, it heals you first. It shows you that you were never broken, only covered. But then that same light begins to illuminate the path for others.

Not because you set out to save them, or because you have to become anyone's teacher, but because when something is truly alive in you, it radiates, like a flower that doesn't try to perfume the air but does so because that is its nature. Your liberation becomes a field, your inner permission becomes an invitation, and your embodied truth becomes light for others.

Many spiritual traditions speak of service coming after awakening, and it's worth understanding this clearly. It isn't service as an obligation, as in "now that I've awakened, I have to save everyone." That can become another trap of the ego. The real service after awakening is much simpler and humbler: letting the light through. Not claiming it as yours, not hiding it, not exaggerating it. Just letting it be.

True Humility

I want to pause here, because we sometimes confuse humility with switching ourselves off. True humility doesn't say, "I have no light, I'm nobody, better stay hidden." That isn't humility; it is denial dressed up as virtue. True humility says: this light isn't mine to own; it's mine to let through.

That is the difference between shining from the ego and shining from authenticity. The ego shines to be seen. Authenticity shines because it can no longer hide what it is. And when we shine from that place, from surrender rather than arrogance, serving others doesn't feel like a burden. It is simply what happens when we stop blocking what life wants to radiate through us, like a lamp, like a flower, like a voice that sings because it was born to vibrate.

The Question I Leave You With

So I come back to the beginning: are you weird?

Is there something in you that you hide because you think it has no value, that it's too much, that no one would understand it, or that it would make you look crazy, arrogant or simply too visible? Is there something you love, something that moves you and feels deeply yours, that you keep to yourself because the world doesn't have a box for it yet? Is there something you deeply enjoy, something that flows naturally through you, that you may not do perfectly but that lives in you and makes you feel more like yourself, and are you hiding it because you're afraid of what people will think?

That thing you're hiding, the thing that doesn't fit, the thing that feels too vulnerable or too weird to show: what if that is exactly your gift? Not in spite of being weird, but precisely because it is.

Authenticity doesn't need to be translated to be accepted. It needs to be lived in order to give light. And the world needs your light: not the corrected, reduced, acceptable version of it, but yours, the original one. The weird one.
