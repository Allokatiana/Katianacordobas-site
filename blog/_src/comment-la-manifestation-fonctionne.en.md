# How Manifestation Actually Works: A Grounded Look

People often talk about manifestation as if it were magic, as if you could imagine something strongly enough and the universe would deliver it. But when I look honestly at my own life, I see something much simpler, and much more practical.

Manifestation is happening all the time, even when we don't realize it. It isn't only about getting what we want. It is about the way our inner state shapes what we notice, how we interpret life and how we move through the world. And when that inner state changes, what becomes visible to us changes too.

Before I give examples, I want to be clear about what I mean by manifestation, because I'm not talking about wishful thinking.

### What I Mean by Manifestation

When people say "manifestation," many of them mean what is often called the law of attraction. Some imagine it as a mystical law. For me, it can be understood in a grounded way.

Manifestation is the process through which our beliefs, our emotions, the state of our nervous system, our attention, the meaning we give things and our actions line up and shape our experience of life. It is how our inner world influences what we perceive, what we believe is possible and which paths we can actually see.

So it isn't only about choosing one desire and trying to attract it. We are always "manifesting," in the sense that we are always filtering reality, giving it meaning and responding to it from a certain inner state, and that state has an effect on what happens around us. A simple example makes this very clear.

### Living in the Same City, Seeing Two Different Worlds

Recently I was talking with a young man and his mother who had just moved to the city where I live.

He told me that people here seem very angry. When he drives, he notices aggressive drivers cutting others off, honking, yelling and looking upset. When he goes for a walk or goes skiing, he feels that people don't say hi and seem bothered or unfriendly. Someone else in the room even said that if you say hi to people here, you should be careful, because they might react angrily.

I remember thinking how interesting this was, because I live in the same city and I don't experience that world. When I go skiing, people often say hello. When I walk in the park, people smile. When I drive, most of what I notice feels normal. Of course, sometimes someone honks or seems impatient. But when that happens, I don't conclude that people are hostile. I simply assume they may be in a hurry, stressed, or going through something difficult. I've been there too.

So there we were, describing the same place and living in two different realities.

### The Mind Looks for Confirmation

This made me reflect on something very human: the mind looks for confirmation of what it already believes.

If someone believes people here are angry, the mind will notice every angry face, every honk, every aggressive reaction. Those moments become evidence; they stand out more and are remembered more. If someone believes people are generally kind, the mind will notice the smiles, the greetings and the small gestures, and those moments become evidence too.

It's not that one person is lying and the other is right. The mind filters reality through the lens it already carries, often unconsciously. In a way, it protects its ideas because it believes they keep us safe.

It reminds me of when I was pregnant: suddenly I saw pregnant women everywhere. They had always been there, but now my attention was tuned to notice them.

This is one of the most grounded ways to understand what people call the law of attraction. Our beliefs shape what we notice, what we focus on, what we consider important and how we respond.

### The Filter Is Not Only Mental

This filter isn't only mental. It is also emotional and physiological, and I noticed something about myself that made this very clear.

I'm not usually someone who feels a lot of anger. But when I do feel angry, I start noticing more angry people around me, especially on the road: more honking, more aggressive driving, more irritation. And in those moments, I change too. Maybe I drive more abruptly, become less patient, react more. So I'm not only noticing more anger; I may also be taking part in the same emotional atmosphere.

On other days, those same angry people may be out there, but I don't even see them. I'm not looking for that confirmation. I'm in a happier place, and my attention naturally goes elsewhere.

It's the same when you're in love. The world looks beautiful, life feels lighter and people seem nicer. Not because reality suddenly became perfect, but because your inner state changed the way you experience it.

So our inner state influences not only what we see but also how we interact, and the way we interact can make an experience better or make it harder.

### Why Trust Changes What Happens

This is why I think trust matters so much, and why we hear phrases like "trust the process."

Trust isn't magic, but it changes the nervous system. When we trust, we relax. The nervous system calms down, the mind becomes clearer, and we think more creatively. We notice opportunities more easily and can see our options.

When we don't trust, when we are anxious or afraid, the nervous system goes into survival mode. The brain focuses on threats, our thinking narrows and we scan for danger. In that state, it is much harder to see solutions. So trust doesn't magically create results; it creates the inner conditions in which solutions become visible.

And there is something important to add: trust isn't only about what we want. It is about what we are actually living inside: our sensations, perceptions, fears and clarity in the moment. You can say "I trust," but if your body is tense, your mind is worried and your system feels unsafe, you are not really living trust. You are living fear, and that affects what you can perceive.

### You Manifest What You Are

This is why people say something I find very true: you don't manifest what you wish for, you manifest what you are. I would put it this way: you don't manifest what you'd like to think, but what is truly integrated inside you.

The way you experience yourself shapes the way you interpret and engage with the world. So manifestation isn't just hoping. It is about what is real in you: your beliefs, your emotional patterns, the state of your nervous system and the meaning you give to things.

### Words and Real Belief

This is where many people misunderstand manifestation.

Some people repeat affirmations, and some repeat Bible verses: "I am safe," "I am protected," "God is with me," "Everything is working out," "I am abundant." I'm not against that. Feeding the mind with better thoughts can be a powerful practice, and words matter.

But the transformation becomes real when those words become true inside us. If I repeat "I am safe" while my nervous system still believes the world is dangerous, the deeper belief will guide my perception. So it isn't only about the words. It is about integration, inner healing, and becoming coherent inside.

### Inner Coherence

Inner coherence means that our thoughts, emotions, beliefs and actions point in the same direction.

An incoherent mind is a divided mind: one part wants something while another fears it; one part desires while another judges or resists. That division creates stress and confusion and reduces clarity. People can want money but believe money is bad, want love but believe relationships always end badly, or want success but believe they aren't capable. That inner conflict shapes what they notice and how they act.

When coherence grows, clarity grows, and when clarity grows, the path becomes more visible.

### Money as an Example

This is very clear with money, especially today, when many young people want to be rich.

Someone can repeat "I am rich" or "I attract money," but if deep down they believe rich people are bad, that money is dirty, or that being rich means taking advantage of others, there is a conflict, and that conflict blocks alignment.

A person who is truly aligned with wealth usually has a different inner relationship with money. Money feels neutral, even friendly. They believe they can create value and that solutions exist, and they naturally look for opportunities. Again, it isn't the words; it's the inner beliefs.

### Obstacles Are Part of the Path

When you truly believe in something, not in a magical way but in a coherent way, you become open to possibilities. Obstacles still appear, because life always has obstacles, but the way you see them changes.

If you believe in the path, you don't take an obstacle as the end. You see it like a hurdle in a race: you go around it, climb it or jump over it, you learn, and you keep moving.

This connects with a phrase attributed to Jesus: everything is possible for the one who believes. To me, that doesn't mean fantasy. It means that real belief changes what you see and how you respond, and keeps you oriented toward solutions.

### A Personal Example: My Husband Becoming a Doctor in Canada

A meaningful example of this in my own life is when my husband and I came to Canada.

He was already a doctor in Colombia. People told us it was extremely difficult to become a doctor here and that very few people manage to do it. Before we came, I did some research and saw that it could take about five years. So I accepted something simple: it would probably be hard, and it might take time. But inside, I stayed oriented toward the belief that a path existed.

That didn't make it easy. It was a demanding process, with exams, requirements, steps, and moments when things didn't work at first. But we stayed open: open to learning what needed to be done, to working hard, and to insisting and continuing even when something didn't work the first time. And step by step, doors appeared, through people, through information and through opportunities that became visible because we kept walking.

It took five years for him to be accepted into a residency program. Five years, exactly what I had prepared for. Interesting, isn't it? Today he has been working as a doctor in Canada for many years.

When I look back, I see this as manifestation. Not because the universe magically did it for us, but because belief kept us open, persistence kept us moving, and that openness allowed us to see the next step.

### How Perception Actually Changes

At this point, you might ask: okay, but how do I change what I truly believe inside? Because we don't change just by deciding to. We change through inner work.

Manifestation isn't only "I choose a new thought." It is healing, observing, and understanding what created the belief in the first place. It is seeing the fear and the protective mechanism underneath. Very often we don't even realize we have a lens. We think we are seeing reality as it is, without realizing that we are seeing it through fear, through old experiences, and through a nervous system trying to protect us. The real work is to become conscious of that.

### Questions That Reveal Your Inner Lens

One of the most powerful ways to transform perception is to begin asking honest questions. When something in the world disturbs me, I can ask:

*Does this also exist inside me?*

*Why does this trigger such a strong reaction in me?*

*Where did this belief about people or life come from?*

*Is what my mind is telling me absolutely true?*

*Could there be another way to see this?*

These questions are not meant to judge ourselves. They help us observe the inner pattern that is shaping how we see.

### Healing Through Observation and Acceptance

When we discover emotions in ourselves such as anger, fear or sadness, our first impulse is often to reject them. But rejecting what is inside us usually makes it stronger.

The deeper path is to observe the emotion, understand it, accept it, and let it be there long enough to reveal what it is protecting. And as the lens through which we see life slowly changes, the world we experience changes too.

### Seeing the Problem and Seeing the Light

This is also deeply connected to how I work with people.

When someone comes to see me, they often arrive focused on the problem. I do see the problem; I want to see what is real so I don't bypass it. But I don't stay only there. I focus more on the part of them that has light, capacity, solutions and potential. Not because I'm pretending the problem doesn't exist, but because I know that part exists too.

Often I ask myself: how can this shadow become light? How can this difficult feeling carry the seed of clarity? When I see that, my attention tunes into it. I begin to see it more clearly, I reflect it back to the person, and together we find the path of healing: the next step, the right technique, the right perspective.

When clarity appears, chaos begins to fade. Not because life becomes perfect, but because we begin to see reality through less of a trauma filter and with more truth.

### Manifestation Is the Way We Meet Life

So manifestation is not about rose-colored glasses, bypassing reality, or repeating phrases and hoping the universe is listening. Manifestation is the way we meet life. It is attention and meaning, our inner state, the regulation of our nervous system, trust and coherence. It is the inner work that turns a belief into something real in the body.

When belief is real and aligned, you become open to possibilities. You see solutions, you handle obstacles differently, and you keep walking. And little by little, what you couldn't see before becomes visible.

So maybe the most powerful question isn't only "How do I attract what I want?" Maybe it is this: *Through what lens am I seeing life right now, and what inner work would help that lens become clearer?*
