# Seeing People As They Are, Not As You Wish They Were

For much of my life, I believed that seeing the best in people was my strength. And while this way of seeing brought light into many situations, it also led me to ignore realities that were right in front of me. I learned this the hard way, in a relationship where I kept focusing on the "light" in someone who was treating me badly. I overlooked their harmful behavior, telling myself, "They're good at heart," until the pain became too much to ignore.

This experience taught me something essential: to heal and grow, we need to learn to see situations, and people, as they truly are, not as we wish them to be.

### Why Objectivity Matters

When we look at others through the lens of what we hope to see, we miss the chance to understand both them and ourselves. This is not about judging someone or labeling them as good or bad. It is about observing their actions and how they make us feel, without filtering everything through our expectations or desires.

For example, if someone yells at you, instead of telling yourself, "Oh, they're just stressed," you can notice the objective reality: "This person is yelling, and it makes me feel afraid." And if you feel fear or unease around someone, you don't have to dismiss it. You can ask yourself, "Why do I feel this way? Is this coming from the present moment, or from something unresolved in my past?"

> This kind of observation is not only about the other person. It is also a mirror of something deeper within you, and every reaction becomes an invitation to heal.

### The Role of the Inner Child

Many of these feelings lead back to our inner child. Let's say someone yells at you and you feel powerless or scared. That reaction might come from a time in your childhood when a caregiver's anger made you feel small, trapped and unable to get away. Back then, you may have learned to swallow your feelings and stay, because leaving was not an option.

As an adult, that same inner child may still be afraid to stand up for themselves, may believe they must "love unconditionally" to avoid conflict, or may feel it is easier, and safer, to tolerate poor treatment than to leave. When you recognize this, healing can begin. You can reassure that inner child: "You are safe now. You are strong, and you have the power to choose what is right for you."

### Healing and Making Conscious Choices

Once you have acknowledged the objective reality of a situation and explored the feelings it stirs in you, you are in a position to make conscious decisions. Maybe you want to take some distance from someone whose behavior hurts you. Maybe an honest conversation about how you feel is needed. Or maybe it is time to walk away entirely. The difference is that now you are not reacting from a wounded, unconscious place; you are choosing from a grounded, healed and empowered part of yourself.

### Why This Matters

Seeing people as they are, and allowing yourself to feel what is true, opens the door to deep healing. It is not about fixing the other person, or yourself. It is about welcoming what is, understanding the lessons it brings, and growing from them.

When we stop forcing ourselves to see what we think we should see, we free ourselves to live more authentically. And in that authenticity lies the power to heal, to transform, and to choose a path that truly aligns with who we are.
