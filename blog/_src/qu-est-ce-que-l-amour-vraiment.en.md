# What Is Love, Really?

When we talk about love, we often reduce it to something romantic, beautiful or pleasant, something that simply makes us feel good. All of that can be part of love, but it isn't enough to explain it.

In this article, I would like to gently remove some of the filters through which we usually see love, so we can come a little closer to what it truly is: in the way we love another person, the way we love ourselves, the way we understand God, and the way we live life.

We use the word love all the time, yet we rarely stop to ask what it actually means. We say "I love this person," "I want to feel loved," "I need self-love," "God is love." And very often, without realizing it, we mix into that word emotion, attachment, attraction, need, habit, the desire to be chosen or the wish not to be alone. That is why it's worth asking, slowly and honestly: what does it really mean to love?

Perhaps love is the ability to see the reality of another person, of ourselves or of life as it is, and from there, to open ourselves to understand, respond and create with more truth. That idea changes the way we understand love, because then love is not only about feeling something beautiful. It is also about being able to truly see.

### Love Sees the Other as They Are

To truly love is to see another person in a fuller reality, and to accept that reality as it is before trying to change it. I know that sounds demanding.

Very often we think we love a person when, in reality, we partly love the idea we have created of them. We love what they could become, the way they made us feel at the beginning, the version of them that fits the life we imagined, the story our mind built. But we are not yet fully accepting who they really are.

Then time passes, and we begin to see real things: limits, wounds, contradictions, unusual traits, habits we don't like, and ways of loving that differ from our own. That is when the inner friction begins, and an important question appears: were we loving the real person, or the version we created in our mind?

To love deeply is to see another person in a wider reality, holding their light, their limits, their contradictions and their real way of being in the same gaze. It means something simple and demanding at the same time: starting from reality, seeing what is actually in front of us, and relating to it honestly.

A simple example helps. When my children were small, my husband and I could see even their clumsiness, their crying or their emotional outbursts with tenderness. Everything was held inside that field of love. But that didn't mean we stopped guiding them, teaching them, correcting certain things or making decisions for their well-being. On the contrary, it is precisely because there is love that there is also a movement toward care, harmony and growth.

This helps us understand something important: love receives what is, and from there, it moves. Love cares, guides, and moves toward truth, balance and the well-being of life. Very often, before we truly see, we are already reacting, judging or trying to correct from the image in our mind, and in that moment we stop meeting the other person as they really are.

### Love Also Means Caring for Yourself in Truth

It is important to say this clearly: loving someone also includes being able to care for yourself.

Sometimes people confuse love with endurance, as if loving meant accepting everything, always giving in, or betraying ourselves so as not to lose the other person. In many of those cases, what is guiding us is no longer love, but fear, dependency, attachment or confusion.

Love also includes honoring your place, your truth and your limits. You can love someone and still recognize that you can't build a healthy relationship with them. You can love someone and say no, or walk away, or recognize that a situation isn't good for you.

This is also where self-love enters in a deeper way. With another person, sometimes you can love and then leave. But you can't leave yourself. That is where the work becomes deeper: a radical acceptance of what you are, not in order to stay the same forever, but because real transformation can only begin from that acceptance.

### Acceptance Opens the Space to See and Choose

Acceptance means seeing that something is as it is. It is recognizing reality and opening an inner space to relate to it with more clarity. Resignation, on the other hand, is living through something with heaviness, feeling that you have no real choice but to endure it. One brings clarity; the other crushes.

Acceptance has more strength than resignation, even if at first it may seem the opposite. When I accept, I finally see more clearly, and when I see more clearly, I can choose more truthfully. If I accept that a person is as they are, I am no longer struggling with a fantasy; I am seeing reality. From there, I can ask: Does this resonate with me? Do I want to stay? Is this healthy for me?

The same is true within myself. If I see a difficult part of myself and observe it honestly, I already have a real chance to understand it, and only what I truly understand can be deeply transformed.

Love moves toward transformation and growth. But it doesn't do that by denying reality; it does it by working from reality. It sees what is there first, and then it works with it. That is why acceptance is not the end of movement, but its right beginning.

### Love Accompanies Pain and Eases Suffering

Love doesn't erase the pain of life. Sometimes we lose something, someone disappoints us, a truth breaks an illusion, and it hurts. Sometimes growth itself hurts. Pain exists.

But there is a difference between pain and the suffering we add when we fill it with inner struggle, mental stories and resistance. When something hurts and we add tension and inner argument, the pain becomes heavier. When something hurts and we can say "yes, this hurt me," without running from it, denying it or amplifying it, something changes. The pain is still there, but there is less war inside.

That is why love can accompany pain and ease suffering: it creates space to feel what we are living and to stay with it more truthfully.

### Unconditional Love Begins When We See Truly

At the beginning of many relationships, there is a great openness. The other person fascinates us, everything feels special, everything seems to flow. Then, over time, that openness gets mixed with our wounds, expectations, fears, ideas and needs. We begin to love the idea we had more than the reality in front of us. We want the other person to love us in a certain way, to act according to what we believe is right, and to change certain parts of themselves so we can feel safer. That is how love begins to become conditional.

Of course, we all have preferences. We all prefer certain ways of loving and being loved, and that is human. But a preference is not a universal law. My preferences are human; reality unfolds with a wider intelligence than my expectations. When I forget that, suffering grows.

### Love Opens and Brings Clarity

Many traditions have said that love and fear move us in different directions, and there is deep truth in that. Love opens; it looks at what is here and makes room for a clearer response. Fear tries to protect us too soon, which is why it so often wants to run, control or deny. It fears loss, fears being hurt, fears not receiving what it hoped for. Love, even when it also feels pain, begins by seeing reality and entering into relationship with it.

And that changes everything. When I see first, I can respond better. When I defend myself before I see, I react from my stories rather than from truth.

### What Does It Mean to Say That God Is Love?

This phrase has been repeated so often that we sometimes no longer pause to reflect on it: "God is love." Many people hear it as if God only gave what is beautiful, gentle and comforting, as if love were the same as comfort.

But perhaps the phrase points to something deeper. Perhaps saying that God is love means that reality itself is held in a space where everything that exists, all of creation, is allowed to exist, to be seen, to be held and to be transformed. Not because everything always feels beautiful or easy, but because everything that exists is included in the reality of being. Life and loss, change, beauty and mystery, what we understand and what we don't yet understand: all of it unfolds within a greater reality that holds existence.

Seen this way, the love of God would not only be comfort or pleasant things. It would be something deeper: a total openness to being, a presence that includes reality and creates from it.

### Love Creates From Reality

From the moment we are born, we seek love. Children, teenagers and adults alike want to love and be loved, to create what we love, to belong and to feel connected. That is why love is not a secondary theme in human life. It is central, a force that moves relationships, choices, wounds, transformation and creation itself.

This leads to one of the most important ideas in this whole reflection: only what has been accepted can be truly transformed. When we reject what is here, resistance appears, and with it usually come more rigidity, more inner struggle and less clarity.

Think of a painter. If a painter rejects the colors they have, they may create nothing at all, or paint from frustration. But if they look at the colors they do have, accept them and work with them, something beautiful can be created from that very reality. Life often works the same way: we create when we see reality and work with it. That is why love doesn't only unite; it also creates. It receives what is here and, from there, opens the possibility for something new to emerge.

### The Different Expressions of One Love

To love another is to see them as they are and accompany their reality without immediately turning them into a project to fix. To love yourself is to look at yourself with honesty, understanding and presence while you keep learning how to transform. To love God is to open yourself to a greater reality and learn to recognize that presence in what is. And to love life is to stay in contact with what is real, to listen to it and to respond with more truth.

Perhaps these are not separate realities. Perhaps they are one movement, expressing itself at different levels.

### So, What Is Love?

If I had to say it as simply as possible, I would say this: love is seeing reality as it is, with deep acceptance, without trying to control it, and responding from there with truth. It means making space to see first, to understand more deeply and to respond more truthfully.

Even though that may sound simple, it changes everything. When you truly see, you understand more clearly; when you understand more clearly, you choose more wisely; and when you choose more wisely, you no longer love only from illusion, fear or need. You love from truth.
