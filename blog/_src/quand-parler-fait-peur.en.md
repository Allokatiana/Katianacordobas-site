# When Speaking Feels Unsafe: The Hidden Wound Around Your Voice

Have you ever been told that you talk too much? Or that you don't speak enough? Have you ever felt judged for being too expressive, or for being too quiet?

Many people carry a wound around the voice without realizing it. It doesn't only show up in what they say, but in the tension around saying it: the hesitation before speaking, the shame afterward, the pressure to talk when they don't want to, or the fear of staying silent when silence would actually feel more true.

For some people, speaking feels dangerous. For others, it is silence that feels dangerous, and for many, both feel loaded. That is why the question is not only whether you speak a lot or a little. The deeper question is where your speaking, or your silence, is coming from.

### The Wound Is Not Just About Words

Some people were told they were too talkative, too loud, too emotional, too much. Others were told they needed to speak up, participate more and stop being so shy. Sometimes the same person received both messages: speak, but not too much; express yourself, but not like that; be present, but don't take up too much space.

Over time, the voice becomes confused. Speaking no longer feels natural, and silence no longer feels free. Both become charged, as if someone were always watching, and both start to carry shame or pressure.

A person may speak and immediately feel regret, or ask a question and then feel guilty for interrupting. They may want to stay quiet but feel they have to prove they are engaged, so they fill the silence, not because they have something to say, but because the silence feels wrong. Or they may hold back, not because they are at peace, but because some part of them expects to be criticized.

This is often how the wound around the voice begins to live inside the body.

### Different Parts of Us Relate to the Voice in Different Ways

When I speak here about "parts," I simply mean that inside us there can be different inner responses and impulses that don't always agree with each other.

One part of us may want to share, ask, respond or think out loud. Another may be naturally quiet and feel no need to speak. Another may try to protect us from judgment, either by pushing us to talk or by stopping us. Another may still carry the humiliation of having been made wrong in the past. And another may be wise enough to pause and ask what is actually true for us in this moment.

These parts are not a problem; they are part of being human. We are not always moved by a single voice inside, and understanding this can help us bring more compassion and clarity to what happens around our voice. Here are some of the parts that often appear when there is a wound around the voice.

### The Part That Wants to Express

In many people there is a part that wants to speak, participate, laugh, think out loud or say what feels true. There is nothing wrong with this part. It is often alive and spontaneous, and it wants to join the moment. It wants to say: I am here, I have something to share, I care.

But when this part has been corrected too often, it may stop feeling safe, and then it can start coming out with urgency. It may speak too quickly or too intensely, not because it is bad, but because it is afraid the space will close. What looks like "too much" is sometimes simply expression that never felt truly safe.

### The Part That Is Naturally Quiet

Some of us also have a part that is quiet by nature. This part isn't ashamed, frozen or absent. It simply doesn't always need to speak. It may enjoy listening, observing and taking things in, and it can be fully present and connected without having to prove it through words. For this part, silence is not a failure; it can feel restful and true.

There is nothing wrong with this part either. The problem begins when silence is no longer a choice, but something judged from the outside or pressured from the inside.

### The Protective Part

There is often another part that tries to protect us from the pain of being judged, embarrassed or rejected. It may say: don't say that, stop talking, you're too much, you're taking up too much space. But to the quiet part it may say the opposite: say something, or people will think you're awkward, or that you have nothing to offer.

This part can sound harsh, but it usually formed for a reason. It learned from experience that speaking could bring shame, and that silence could bring shame too. So it doesn't trust freedom; it trusts control. Its job is to keep you safe, but sometimes it does that by making both your voice and your silence feel unsafe.

### The Humiliated Part

Underneath all this, there is often a more vulnerable part: the one that remembers what it felt like to be made wrong. It was made wrong for talking too much, and sometimes for not talking enough; for being too expressive, or for being too quiet.

This part may say: I should have spoken more, now they think I'm not smart. Or: I should have stayed quiet, now they think I'm too much. It carries the pain of having been seen through shame, and it can show up as guilt after speaking, regret after staying silent, a tight throat, discomfort in the body or a wish to disappear. It is the part that still feels the wound most directly.

Many people live from this place without realizing it. They think it is just their personality, but sometimes what feels like personality is actually adaptation.

### The Wise Part That Can Choose

Healing begins when another part grows stronger: the wise one. This is the part that can notice what is happening without immediately obeying fear, pressure or habit. It can ask:

*Do I want to speak right now, or do I genuinely want to stay quiet?*

*Am I expressing myself from truth, or from anxiety?*

*Am I silent from peace, or from shame?*

This part doesn't force speech and doesn't force silence. It brings back choice. And that matters, because healing is not about becoming more talkative or more quiet. It is about becoming more conscious of where your expression is coming from.

### Sometimes You See the Wound in the Mirror

One of the clearest signs that you may carry a wound around the voice is how strongly you react to the way other people express themselves.

You may see someone talking a lot and saying what they think, and feel irritated: why do they talk so much, why don't they stop? Or you may see someone very quiet and judge that too: why don't they say anything, do they have nothing to say?

Sometimes the criticism we feel toward others is not really about them. It is the old voice we heard before, now turned outward. The person who speaks too much may reflect the part of you that was shamed for expressing yourself, and the person who speaks too little may reflect the part of you that was shamed for staying quiet. What annoys us in others can show us a wound we haven't yet fully recognized in ourselves.

### It Is Not About Finding the Perfect Balance

The point is not to find one correct amount of speaking, or to talk less because someone finds you intense, or more because silence makes others uncomfortable. The real question is not "Am I talking too much or too little?" It is **"Where is this coming from?"**

*Am I speaking because it feels true, or because I'm afraid of disappearing?*

*Am I staying quiet because I feel peaceful, or because I'm ashamed?*

*Am I filling the silence because I think I should?*

*Am I holding back what is alive in me because I expect criticism?*

That is where consciousness begins.

### You Have the Right to Speak, and You Have the Right to Be Silent

This may sound simple, but for many people it is deeply healing to hear.

You have the right to speak, and you have the right to stay silent.

You have the right to ask, and the right to pause.

You have the right to be expressive, and the right not to perform.

You have the right not to fill every silence, and not to shrink because someone is uncomfortable with your presence.

And if there is a moment when you don't want to speak, but a part of you feels it is necessary, you can still choose to speak. Just do it consciously, knowing that you are the one choosing it and feeling where it comes from. And if you do want to speak, then speak, not as a performance or a defense, but as an honest expression of what feels true.

The freedom is not in talking more or less. It is in no longer being unconsciously ruled by the wound.

### Returning the Voice to Freedom

Many people have never really been given full permission with their voice. They were corrected when they expressed too much and pushed when they didn't express enough, so they learned to manage themselves constantly: to edit, anticipate, hold back, explain or disappear. But the voice was never meant to feel like a test, and silence was never meant to be a failure.

Maybe healing this wound is not about becoming someone else. Maybe it is about becoming honest enough to notice what is moving you, and to hear each of these parts: the one that wants to express, the one that is naturally quiet, the one that protects, the one that still feels humiliated, and the one that is wise enough to choose. Not perfectly, but consciously.

In the end, the deepest freedom is not in learning how to speak correctly, but in knowing that your voice and your silence can both belong to you.
