# If You Think You Know Who You Are, You Might Not

We often hear "Be yourself." It sounds simple, even obvious. But who is that? Who is this "yourself" you're supposed to be?

Most people believe they know. They say "I'm like this," and point to a set of thoughts, habits, reactions or personality traits. But that isn't all of who you are. It's a flicker, a passing light on the surface of the ocean.

The truth is that much of what we call our identity is a bundle of ideas we received from the world around us: beliefs, expectations, cultural codes. From a very young age we are told who we are, and one day we believe it.

And the moment we believe we have found who we are, we often become prisoners of that idea. We spend our lives protecting it, defending it, proving it and trying to live up to it, even when it no longer feels true. That is where suffering begins, because what we are protecting is no longer who we are, and we are too busy protecting it to notice what we are becoming.

### Authenticity Is Not a Fixed Thing

We often confuse authenticity with consistency. We say "I'm just being myself," when what we really mean is "I'm being who I decided I am." But the part of us that thinks it knows who we are is often just repeating what it was taught.

True authenticity isn't a fixed thing. It is a living permission, the courage to keep unfolding. It is a willingness to let go of every label, every box and every fixed story, and to listen instead to the quiet, moving truth inside. You are not a definition. You are a becoming.

### Becoming Is a Return

Look at the word itself in the languages I live in. In Spanish, *volverse* comes from *volver*, to return. In French, *devenir* means to come into being. In English, *become* joins "be" and "come," as if something were coming into what it already is.

Each of these languages points toward the same mystery: to become is to return. Not to turn into someone else, but to unfold into the one you have always been, before the masks, before the roles, before the forgetting. It isn't about changing who you are; it is about remembering.

### Like a Photograph in Water

Think of an old photograph being developed in its bath. At first you see nothing but shadows. Then, slowly, a face begins to appear: lines, features, light. You don't add anything; you simply let the image emerge.

That is what becoming truly is. You are the photograph, and you are the water. And when the image becomes clear, you realize something astonishing: the picture is alive, and it will never stop changing.

### Seeing Only a Small Part of Our Light

You are so much more than what you can see. In this human experience, we perceive only a tiny fragment of ourselves at a time: a glimmer, a whisper, a single point of light among countless stars.

I don't see that as a flaw. I see it as the very design of experience. We live second by second, moment by moment, because that is how we can feel life. If we saw everything at once, we couldn't hold it, and we wouldn't even know how to love it.

And maybe this limit isn't only there because we couldn't handle more. Maybe our Creator is whispering: "Let's not rush. Let's savor the becoming." Life is like a lover who wants to be met slowly, with full presence, not all at once. In the small, sacred portion we are given, we find a whole universe. We are given one breath, one step, one sliver of light, and within it we can touch the infinite. It is small and vast at the same time, and that is what makes it holy.

### We Are Not an Instant, We Are Time Unfolding

Sometimes we say, "It's 8 a.m." But the moment we say it, it's no longer true. It's 8 o'clock and one second, then two, then three. Time keeps moving; we can't hold it or capture it. It always slips away, like a mirage.

We are exactly like that. We imagine ourselves as someone fixed in a moment, as if we could define ourselves with a single image or idea. But a second later, we are already no longer the same; we are already changing.

Time stretches through past, present and future, and while we can only experience it one moment at a time, we are the whole line. We move along it without ever seeing it all, and yet we are made of it: every point, every second, every transformation. Just as we can't hold time in our hands, we can't trap who we are in a concept. We are not a moment or a label. We are time itself, unfolding in awareness.

### A Living Movement

So who are you? Not a name, a role, a story or a conclusion. You are a becoming. Like the tide, the wind or a flame, you are always in motion, and so is your truth.

Even "now" isn't still; this moment is already changing. And yet it is the only place where your being can be felt. Not in memory, not in imagination, but here and now. That is where the real you lives.

So when someone asks you, "Who are you?", you might pause, smile, and say: "I am what is becoming. I am the unfolding. I am the mystery remembering itself." And maybe that's all we need to know: not to define the mystery, but to become it.

### And If You Look Deeper

And if you truly begin to ask, "Who am I, really?", the most honest answer you'll find is: I don't know.

Because you can't know who you are with the mind. You are constantly discovering yourself, moment by moment, in a movement that never ends. Who you are can never be captured or fixed. It can only be experienced and lived. You can only be the "I am." You can't hold it, explain it or finish it.

That is why we can only speak of it the way I am doing now: in the flow of being, with love, and with no need to define it.
