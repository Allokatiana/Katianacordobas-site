# We Are Fractals

Have you ever heard of a fractal? A fractal is a shape made of smaller shapes that look like the whole. If you zoom in, each of those smaller shapes is made of the same pattern, and if you zoom in again, the pattern keeps repeating. It is a pattern that contains itself at every scale.

That isn't just mathematics; it's life. We are fractals. What surrounds us is built in a way that echoes what is inside us, and what we see is not separate from us, but a living mirror of our own structure.

### Fractals in Nature

Fractals appear everywhere in nature. Look at a tree: the trunk splits into big branches, the branches into smaller ones, then into twigs, and each branch is like a little tree inside the bigger one. Look at a river: it divides into tributaries, then streams, then little channels. Look at lightning: one bolt branches, and branches again, much like the veins of a leaf.

Leaves themselves are fractals. Look closely at one and you'll see a central vein, secondary veins branching off it, and smaller veins branching off those. It's the same pattern as the tree's branches, in miniature; the leaf is like a little tree within the tree. Nature uses the same geometry to organize itself at every level.

And it isn't only outside us. Our lungs are like upside-down trees: the trachea divides into bronchi, then into smaller bronchioles, and finally into tiny air sacs. Our blood vessels branch like rivers, and our neurons branch like lightning. Life repeats its patterns everywhere, like a signature of creation.

### From the Atom to the Cosmos

When we go even smaller, into the atom, the same echo appears. An atom is almost entirely empty space. At its center is the nucleus, made of protons and neutrons, and inside each of those are quarks, held together by gluons. Physicists don't describe these as solid little balls, but as energy in constant motion, bound together by invisible fields.

At the other end of the scale, the universe is also mostly empty space. Galaxies gather along immense filaments, forming what astronomers call the cosmic web, with vast voids in between. At the tiniest scale and at the largest, we find something similar: mostly emptiness, with points of matter linked by connections.

Our brains echo this pattern too. A network of billions of neurons connected by fine threads can look strikingly like a map of galaxies, and some researchers have even compared the two and found surprising structural similarities. The very small, the very large and the organ of our own consciousness seem to mirror one another.

This is the ancient principle "as above, so below," and it's what makes the image of the fractal so powerful for me. The pattern at the smallest scale is reflected at the largest, and the largest is reflected back in us.

### In His Image

Sacred texts say that God made us in His own image. What if that image is not a physical shape, but a pattern, a living fractal of divine energy? God dividing Himself into parts of Himself that are still Himself, the way a first cell divides into two identical cells that keep multiplying and shaping life. Each cell holds the essence of the first one, and that first cell, the origin, carries the code of all creation.

From our human perspective, we don't see this. We are like a tiny piece of a fractal looking at another tiny piece, not realizing that both belong to the same whole. We see separation because we can't see the whole pattern from where we stand. But everything we see, people, nature, objects and stars, is a piece of the same pattern, and in that sense, a part of ourselves.

If we could rise to a higher point of view, we would see life as it is: an endless, living fractal, the One repeating itself in infinite ways. We would understand that when we transform ourselves, the pattern around us transforms too. Physics offers a beautiful image of this. Two entangled particles remain linked even when they are far apart: what is measured in one is connected to what is found in the other. Einstein called it "spooky action at a distance." For me, it is an image of what we are: expressions of the same Source, connected beyond distance. When you heal, the mirror of reality shifts. When you open your heart, the world resonates.

This is why knowing yourself is also knowing God. His breath is within you and His spirit moves through you. We are in Him, and He is in us.

To see the world as it truly is, a living fractal of love, consciousness and creation, is to step out of confusion and into recognition. In that seeing, something opens, and the light we have been searching for outside begins to shine from within. We remember what was always true: we are not just living in a universe of copies. We are the universe looking at itself.
