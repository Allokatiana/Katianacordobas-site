Is this you?

Maybe you've been taking care of everything, except yourself.

You manage your responsibilities, care for the people you love, and keep going, even when things aren't easy. But somewhere along the way, you may have forgotten to ask yourself how you're really doing.

Maybe some of this feels familiar:

  • You feel overwhelmed, anxious, or stressed, and sometimes you don't understand why.
  • You're always there for others, but rarely make time for yourself.
  • You find it difficult to understand or express what you're feeling.
  • You notice the same patterns repeating in your relationships or the choices you make.
  • You're going through a loss, a difficult relationship, or a change in your life, and you're trying to find your way.

Or maybe nothing is particularly wrong. You're simply curious about yourself. You want to understand yourself better, explore what's happening inside you, or discover another way of experiencing life.

Whatever brings you here, you don't need to have everything figured out. You can come exactly as you are.

Read my story

Ever since I was a little girl, I've asked myself many questions about life, about God, and about what it means to be human.

I was born in Colombia, into a deeply religious family. As a teenager, I was already teaching religion to children, and I sang in church. My spiritual search was sincere, but it also came with fears, rules, and beliefs I didn't dare to question.

In 2005, I left Colombia to come and live in Quebec. This is where I've built a large part of my life. I'm the mother of two children, and I've shared my life with my husband for almost 30 years.

Like everyone, I've known beautiful moments, but also very difficult times: anxiety, depression, doubt, and moments when I no longer really knew who I was. For a long time, I looked for answers outside myself: in books, in religion, in what other people said.

In 2012, I left religion. And everything changed.

Instead of searching outside, I began to look within. To observe my behaviours, my thoughts, my emotions. I began to meditate, to connect with myself. I felt that God and I were one, so I no longer needed to look anywhere else. That made all the difference.

I began to see life as a mirror: what surrounded me reflected something of who I was. And I learned to listen to that reflection.

Sound has always been there. In church, when I sang, I would sometimes sing in tongues, a language made of vibrations rather than words. I used to say: this is God's presence. I felt in harmony with everything, with the universe. That connection never left me. I kept singing, meditating with my voice, letting sound guide me.

Then I wanted to go deeper. I trained at a very good school of sound healing, then in neuro-linguistic programming (NLP), and now in hypnosis.

And I continue my inner work, every day. For me, it's the heart of everything. I'm convinced that to accompany someone, you first have to do this work on yourself. Otherwise, we risk projecting our own pain onto the other person, believing they're living the same thing we are. We are one, and at the same time, each of us lives our own experience. The more I do my inner work, the more clearly I see the person in front of me.

I don't have all the answers, and I don't pretend to. I'm still learning, questioning, and discovering too.

Here, you can be completely yourself. And the good news is, you are accepted exactly as you are.

My toolbox

What we may discover together.

I use a variety of approaches in my sessions. There's no fixed formula: we explore together, according to what's happening for you in the moment.

Tap a practice to read about it.

  • Everything starts with a real conversation. You speak freely, and I listen closely to your words. With tools from NLP, we notice how the way you talk about things shapes what you feel.

  • Instead of staying in your thoughts, we turn to your body: its sensations, its tensions, what you feel inside. What are your emotions telling you there? That's often where so much is held.

  • Drums, maracas, Tibetan bowls, crystal bowls, my voice and other instruments, to help your body settle and let go. I also have a vibroacoustic bed: the vibration moves through your whole body, and the relaxation can last even longer.

  • Meeting a younger part of you with kindness, and listening to what it needed. No need to relive painful events.

  • Slowing down to notice your thoughts and emotions without getting lost in them.

  • A deeply relaxed, guided state that can help you reach what's hard to put into words. You stay in control the whole time. My hypnosis is based on my training in transformative hypnosis, accentuated with a more humanist approach.

  • Some things we don't usually notice are there, quietly running our lives: reactions we think we can't control, but that control us. Together, we bring them into the light, so you can see them and choose again.

  • Shamanic-inspired visualization, symbols, contemplation, manifestation and your relationship with Source, for those who wish. No belief required.

These tools can help you know yourself and feel better. They don't replace care from a psychologist or a doctor.

Your questions.

How is this different from seeing a psychologist or psychotherapist?

Psychology and psychotherapy work within a clinical framework. My work focuses on emotional well-being and self-knowledge, through the body and experience. It does not replace psychological care.

What happens in a first session?

We start with what's present for you today. You talk, I listen, and together we see what would help most: conversation, the body, meditation or sound. You only share what you want.

How do I know if this approach is right for me?

If you'd like to understand yourself better, feel calmer, or work with more than just words, it may be a good fit. If you're unsure, text me your question and we'll see together.

Can I come if I'm already seeing a psychologist?

Yes. The two can go well together, and you're always free to keep both.

Do I need to know what I want to work on?

No. Many people arrive with just a feeling that something needs attention. We start there.

What if it's hard for me to talk about my feelings?

That's very common, and it's okay. We can begin with silence, your breath or the singing bowl. Words often come later.

Do I have to talk about my past or my childhood?

No. We start with today. We only look at the past if it comes up and you feel ready.

Do I need to be spiritual or believe in anything?

No. You come as you are, with or without beliefs. If spirituality interests you, we can explore it together.

How long is a session, and what does it cost?

Individual sessions are $130 plus taxes. They usually last 60 to 90 minutes. If we need more time, we take it, sometimes up to two hours, at no extra cost. Couples sessions are $180 plus taxes.

How many sessions will I need?

It depends on you and what you're going through. We decide together, one session at a time.

Can I get a receipt for my insurance?

Yes. I'm a member of the Association des naturothérapeutes du Québec (ANQ), so I can give you a receipt as a naturotherapist (naturothérapeute). Reimbursement depends on your plan.
